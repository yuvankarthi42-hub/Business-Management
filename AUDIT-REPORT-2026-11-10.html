Scenario A — Hotel, 2,000 products, 50 bills/hour: PASS (with the findings below)

One hour of hotel work (50 bills: 30 dine-in with KOT + extra items + settle, 10 counter, 10 QR) ran with 0 failures; capacity on 4 counters was 16,420 bills/hour (target 50). Every hotel page loads in 0.6–1.1 s. Most APIs are under 10 ms CPU at p50; 10 of 155 are over (exports, imports, admin and the Orders list), 33 are over at p95.

Scenario B — Silks / textile, 100,000 products, 1,000 bills/hour: PASS for throughput, with caveats

1,000 bills of 20 items on 10 counters: 0 failures, 11,608 bills/hour (target 1,000), stock consistent afterwards (40/40). Caveats: a device's first Billing visit downloads the index (7.8 s, 17.1 MB; pages over 10 ms CPU — P-1); checkout CPU at 100k is around 10 ms at p50 (P-3); simultaneous-bill bursts queue (P-4); oversold items cannot take small purchases (B-1).
Not a production-readiness claim. Everything ran on one Mac: the real app in its own process, a fake Turso (sql.js behind the Turso HTTP protocol) in another, database latency injected at 0 and 10 ms. No Zoho Catalyst, no real Turso, no real mobile networks or phones. Results show the code's behaviour and relative cost; real latency, Catalyst CPU accounting and libSQL query speed must be confirmed on the platform.

Bottlenecks and recommended fixes (in order)

  1. B-1 allow purchases on oversold items (a textile shop sells pieces it has not entered yet).
  2. P-1/P-2 smaller light-index pages and stock-only deltas — first device setup and busy-day syncs of the 100k shop.
  3. P-3/P-4 fewer round trips in checkout / settle / save (batch the write chain) — keeps 20-item bills under 10 ms at 100k and shortens the write lock in bursts.
  4. P-5 exports as a background job.
  5. B-2, B-3, B-4 small fixes (field types, products.page spec, the index migration); update test T-1.
  6. S-1, S-2 trim the QR menu's billing fields; tracking by code only.
  7. P-6…P-9 Orders list without lines, indexes / stock totals for Inventory at 100k, assignBarcodes, skip events.ticket without a hub, one Dashboard summary call.

Test and infrastructure limitations

  • Local machine only; fake Turso (sql.js) — database query times are sql.js, not libSQL; network latency simulated (0 and 10 ms per round trip), no packet loss.
  • Per-request CPU is exact only for sequential calls; load tests report whole-process CPU ÷ bills (includes background polling) and per-API wall times; per-call DB trip counts under concurrency are not attributable.
  • The paced textile run used a serial 3.6 s wait, so it achieved 888 bills/h instead of exactly 1,000; the burst run shows the real capacity.
  • The Catalyst Data Store path ran the functional test suite only (no load test). WhatsApp, Catalyst cron/cache and web push were not exercised (external services).
  • Browser timings: the app's in-app Chromium on a desktop; phones on mobile data will be slower, especially the 16.7 MB first index download.
  • Product admin, support and WhatsApp settings handlers not measured are listed under the hotel API table.
